No Surprises

Straight Answers and Upfront Prices

No homeowner likes surprises on the final bill. So Grand Prairie Water Heater quotes every job upfront, after a full diagnostic that looks at the heater's age, fuel type, and capacity, the elements or burner, the anode rod and relief valve, and the venting and supply lines. You'll know the problem, what it takes to fix, and what it costs before you say yes.

Most water heater failures send small signals well before the tank gives out. Watch for hot water with a rusty tint, popping noises when the burner fires, a sulfur smell from the hot tap, or a hot water supply that runs out faster than before. Behind those symptoms are usually heavy sediment, a depleted anode rod, or a failing dip tube. An early service call at the first sign often costs far less than mopping up after a ruptured tank.

Do you know how old your water heater is? The serial number on the label usually tells us. When a tank gets past the 10-year mark, it's worth scheduling a replacement before the current one decides for you. As a rough guide, a 40 to 50 gallon gas tank typically runs $1,400–$2,800 installed, and an electric tank typically runs $1,100–$2,400, depending on venting, code updates, and access. Our team will tell you plainly whether yours has years left or is running on borrowed time.

Annual Flushing

Tank Flushing and Tune-Ups That Pay Off

The best repair is the one you never need. That's the whole point of our maintenance plans. Once a year, we drain and flush the tank, measure the anode rod, test the relief valve, inspect the venting and gas connections or the electrical components, and run a descaling cycle on tankless units, all tailored to the heater in your home.

Many homes do best with a yearly flush, and homes with hard water may need a visit every six months. We adjust the schedule to the age of your equipment, how hard your household runs it, and the mineral content of your water, which controls how fast sediment accumulates and how long an anode rod lasts.

Consider maintenance as protection for equipment you've already bought. Each visit helps keep the tank clear of sediment, the anode rod doing its job, and the relief valve ready to open, which lowers monthly energy bills while easing the strain on the burner or elements. For most households, maintenance costs far less than replacing the heater ahead of schedule.

Proper Sizing

A Water Heater Sized for Your Actual Household

An undersized water heater runs out halfway through the second shower, while an oversized one burns fuel keeping gallons you never use hot around the clock. Grand Prairie Water Heater sizes every replacement and new installation to your actual household. For tank models, that means looking at the first-hour rating to your busiest hour, not just copying the gallon size on the old tank. For tankless models, it means sizing the flow rate in GPM to the fixtures you run at once and how cold your incoming water gets in winter.

Picking a new water heater is about more than tank size. We'll compare tankless units against a traditional tank, and gas against electric or hybrid, laying out operating costs, venting and gas line requirements, electrical service, and the installation itself. Tankless is a strong fit for many families, but it often needs a larger gas line and new venting, while a properly sized tank can be the simpler, lower-cost choice. You choose what fits your home and budget.

Common Problems

The Water Heater Repairs Local Homes Need Most

Homes in Grand Prairie run into a familiar set of water heater failures, and each one calls for a different fix. Grand Prairie Water Heater handles them all: no hot water, water that only gets lukewarm, pilots that won't stay lit, electric tanks with burned-out elements, noisy tanks full of sediment, rusty or smelly water, leaking relief valves, and tankless units flashing error codes. Our technicians identify the exact fault and target the repair to the component that actually failed.

Some problems are just a nuisance, while others pose real risks. A burned-out element simply leaves you with lukewarm water, but a relief valve that keeps discharging is a warning that temperature or pressure inside the tank is too high, and a thermostat set above 120°F can burn a child in seconds. If you ever smell gas near the heater, leave the house and call your gas utility from outside before you call us. We match the fix to the failure: elements and thermostats on electric tanks, thermocouples, flame sensors, and gas valves on gas units, a thermal expansion tank where pressure is climbing, and leak repair on drain valves, fittings, and relief lines.
