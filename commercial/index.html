Food Service Hot Water

Hot Water for Kitchens, Restaurants, and Food Facilities

Dish machines, sanitizing sinks, and handwashing stations all compete for the same hot water, and a heater that's undersized or failing shows up first as a dish machine that can't hit temperature and a backed-up dish room. A high-temperature dish machine usually needs final rinse water near 180°F, which usually means a booster heater paired with the main supply. Our food-service work covers high-input tanks, tankless racks, booster heaters, and peak-demand sizing, with service scheduled around your hours.

Hot water temperature is a core issue for any food-service operator. Health inspectors check water temperature at handwashing sinks and sanitizing stations, and a dish machine that can't reach its rinse temperature can stop service. Our work keeps your system delivering the temperatures and volume your operation depends on, with documented service records that demonstrate a well-maintained kitchen to inspectors and landlords. We provide detailed reports after each visit, along with the records many leases and inspectors ask for.

Replacements and maintenance visits are planned with owners, managers, and kitchen staff, so everything fits your operating schedule. When a job can't happen during service, we do it after close, so the kitchen opens the next day with hot water ready.

Code & Inspections

Safe Installations, Documented Service

Badly installed or neglected hot water equipment can create carbon monoxide hazards, scalding, Legionella risk in lukewarm storage, and costly water damage from a ruptured tank. That's a real liability for a commercial property owner responsible for employees, customers, and tenants. Our commercial installations and inspections cover venting, combustion air, gas line sizing, relief valve discharge, expansion tanks, mixing valves, and code items like drain pans and seismic straps where required, followed by a written report that ranks each risk by priority and recommends specific fixes.

This work serves a dual purpose: it protects people and property, and it provides the documentation that demonstrates due diligence to landlords, insurers, and health or building inspectors. We maintain detailed service logs, temperature records, relief valve test dates, and a documentation binder for every account, with more frequent service for heavy-use equipment in kitchens, laundries, and large mechanical rooms.

Ongoing Protection

Scheduled Maintenance for Commercial Hot Water

A maintenance program from Grand Prairie Water Heater gets built around your equipment, how hard the system works, and your budget. Visits include anode checks and tank flushing, descaling of tankless heat exchangers, relief valve testing, burner, flue, and venting inspection, recirculation pump and mixing valve service, and temperature verification at the tap. Hard water shortens the life of commercial equipment quickly, so high-use properties often need quarterly visits.

Compared with an emergency water heater replacement, scheduled maintenance costs a fraction as much, and it sharply cuts the risk of downtime and failed inspections. Properties that stay on a schedule usually see fewer outages, longer equipment life, and lower fuel bills. Give us a call to talk through a plan sized to your property and budget.
