Keeping Your Irving Water Heater Running Longer

Many premature tank failures start with maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Drain and flush the tank once a year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and operate the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Set the thermostat close to 120 degrees for safer water and less scale. If you have a tankless unit, descale it yearly where the water is hard so the heat exchanger stays clean.

Equipment age plays a big role. With regular service, a tank water heater usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like discolored hot water, popping noises, or a damp spot around the base, it's time to plan a replacement. A planned replacement is cheaper and is far less stressful than an emergency swap with water spreading across the floor.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to high water pressure or a missing expansion tank, both modest fixes. The same drip ignored for months can wear out the valve and stress the tank until the tank itself fails, and a small repair becomes a full replacement plus water damage. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.

What Goes Wrong With Local Water Heaters

Most water heater failures we see in Irving start with the water itself. Depending on the neighborhood and the water source, minerals can be a real factor: calcium and magnesium drop out as sediment on the tank floor, insulating the water from the burner, which produces the popping and rumbling you may hear during a heating cycle. In tankless units, the same minerals build scale inside the heat exchanger, and that's why annual descaling matters so much in hard-water areas.

Aging equipment is the other half of the story. A tank-style water heater typically lasts 8 to 12 years, and by then the anode rod is often used up, leaving the tank exposed to corrosion. Homes here also run on a mix of fuel types, natural gas and propane units in some homes and electric in others, so our technicians handle everything from worn thermocouples and sooty burners to tripped high-limit switches and dead elements.

Every house is set up a little differently. A home on a private well can see more sediment and sometimes a sulfur smell in the hot water, often fixed by switching the anode rod type. A tankless heater on hard water needs regular descaling. And an older install getting a new heater may need code items that weren't required when it went in: an expansion tank, a properly routed relief valve line, a drain pan, or seismic straps where required. All of these is fixable, and most are much cheaper when caught early.
